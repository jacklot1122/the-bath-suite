What a full renovation involves

Back to the studs, then forward properly.

Most bathrooms that need renovating do not need a facelift. The shower is in the wrong corner, the vanity door fouls the towel rail, there is nowhere to put anything, and the waterproofing under the floor has been failing quietly for years.

A full renovation strips the room back to studs and joists. That is the only point at which the layout can genuinely change, and it is the moment to move the waste, widen the shower, lose the bulkhead that was never doing anything, or take the tiling to the ceiling.

From there it runs in order: demolition and removal, structural work, plumbing and electrical rough-in, waterproofing to AS 3740, screeding, tiling, joinery, glass and fit-off. One contractor carries all of it, so no stage sits waiting on somebody else's diary.

Before you start

What people ask first.

How long does a full bathroom renovation take?

Weeks rather than days. The honest answer depends on the size of the room, how much structural work is involved, and how long your chosen tile and tapware take to arrive. You get a program with the quote, and we tell you up front if a selection has a long lead time.

What does it cost?

It depends on three things: the size of the room, whether the layout moves, and what you specify. Moving plumbing costs more than keeping it. The quote is itemised so you can see what each decision adds, and we will tell you where money makes a visible difference and where it does not.

Do we have to move out?

Usually not. If it is the only bathroom in the house we will walk you through the sequence and the days without water before you commit, so you can plan around it rather than find out midway.
